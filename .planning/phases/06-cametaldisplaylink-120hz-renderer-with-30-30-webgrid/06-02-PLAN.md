## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Velocity producer thread → VelocityRing → display-link consumer thread | A `CursorVelocity` frame crosses from a producer thread (synthetic now, Rust decoder later) to the renderer's display-link callback thread via the SPSC ring. |
| CursorVelocity (untrusted once the real decoder lands) → CursorIntegrator | The integrator consumes velocity that, in Phase 7, comes from a learned decoder + Kalman — it may emit NaN/Inf or out-of-range values; the integrator is the validation point at the seam. |

## STRIDE Threat Register

| Threat ID | Category | Component | Disposition | Mitigation Plan |
|-----------|----------|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| T-06-02-01 | Tampering / Elevation | CursorIntegrator consuming an unclamped or NaN/Inf `(vx,vy)` from the (future) decoder producer — could index the cursor out of the grid or drive it off-surface | mitigate | `integrate` rejects non-finite velocity (holds position) and clamps the resulting position to `[0,1]` grid bounds (D-04). The post-clamp position is provably finite and in-range, so the kernel's cursor disc + cell mapping never receive an off-grid/NaN coordinate. Unit-tested (Task 1 NaN/Inf + saturation tests). |
| T-06-02-02 | Information Disclosure / Tampering | Torn read / use-after-free across the producer→consumer SPSC seam (a partially-written `CursorVelocity` observed by the consumer) | mitigate | Acquire/Release memory ordering (Task 2 mechanism (a)): producer writes the slot THEN releases the tail index; consumer acquires the tail THEN reads — a half-written frame is never visible. `CursorVelocity` is a trivial value type copied by value (no heap pointers → no use-after-free). Mirrors the Phase-3 loom-verified ring's Release/Acquire discipline. Bounded `push` returns false when full (no overwrite of unconsumed frames). |
| T-06-02-03 | Denial of Service | A producer pushing faster than the consumer pops, filling the ring | accept | `push` returns false when full (the synthetic producer simply drops the surplus; the integrator's zero-order hold + last-velocity behavior keeps the cursor smooth). The 50Hz velocity vs 120Hz render cadence means the consumer drains faster than the producer fills. Documented. |

In-process only (no daemon hop, no network, no persistence, no auth — CONTEXT "Integration Points: in-process producer"). Memory-safety at the ring seam + velocity validity at the integrator are the entire surface. Web-app threats do not apply.
